The partnership

One clean deal — Claire keeps control.

A simple commission on rooms, a straight split on activities, and an open book both ways. Claire owns the house and runs it; African Sky fills it and funds the marketing.

Who earns what

The split.

Claire
80%

of room revenue — plus 50% of every activity margin. Runs the house and carries its operating costs.

African Sky
20%

of room revenue — plus 50% of activity margin. Funds and runs all marketing, sales, web & the booking system.

Accommodation

20% / 80%, one commission.

African Sky earns 20% of room revenue; Claire keeps 80%. That one commission is where we fund the marketing, distribution and booking platform — so Claire always keeps the larger share.

Tours & activities

Split 50/50.

Your operator discount plus our 35% markup, shared evenly. On a $100 rack tour: sell $135, our cost $87.50, margin $47.50 → $23.75 each.

Who pays what

We carry the cost of filling it.

African Sky funds all sales, marketing, website, social and our own booking system. Claire funds the property’s running costs and F&B.

Open book

Total transparency, both ways.

A shared live calendar and a monthly one-page statement — every booking, rate, cost and split visible to both sides. Nothing binding until a proper agreement is signed.

The treatment

Fee income, not an owned asset.

African Sky’s 20% of rooms + 50% of tour margin, net of its marketing spend, converted to Rand and taxed at 27%. It feeds NewCo’s distributable profit and partner distributions — and is deliberately excluded from Valuation / NAV, because it is fee income, not a building we own.

Why it matters

Distributions up, buy-in flat.

Management income needs no capital, so it lifts partner distributions and the cash-on-cash yield without adding a rand to the buy-in. Return on invested capital on this stream is, literally, infinite.
